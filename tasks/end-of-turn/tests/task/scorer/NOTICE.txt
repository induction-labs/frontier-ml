End-of-turn source and evaluation provenance (trusted operator metadata)

Source audio and three independent annotation tracks:
https://huggingface.co/datasets/mundo-ai/turn-benchmark-dev
revision 8fa18a24be51528a45397b35cbcaecd84202062b

Gold construction and EOT metric:
https://github.com/SesameAILabs/turnbench
revision 76ccd045f121ccfa921abac2ad3107027e736911
The gold/floor routines preserve the upstream algorithms. The upstream event
matcher, aggregation and qualification are not used for scoring: the harness
scores a per-frame probability stream against the same gold windows (below).
Linear latency percentiles use a standard-library equivalent of NumPy's
default percentile operation. MIT notices are embedded in both adapted
Python modules.

This task partitions the 38 labelled development conversations into eight
public conversations (1.6255 hours) and thirty evaluation conversations
(5.6820 hours). This is a dev-derived benchmark, not the official test
leaderboard. No inaccessible test labels are inferred or substituted.

The partition uses whole connected speaker groups, target count eight,
conversation-type coverage, and type-frequency distribution. A stable salted
hash resolves ties. Labels, model predictions, and scores are not inputs.
The public IDs are 20,21,22,94,95,122,124,125; their six speakers are disjoint
from the twenty evaluation speakers. Both partitions contain all six types.
The importer audits the partition and byte hashes. Original 48 kHz mono,
24-bit FLAC channels are copied without decoding or resampling. Lossy preview
columns are never model inputs. Data license and attribution travel with both
prepared partitions. The prepared data is mirrored under end-of-turn/ in
inductionlabs/frontier-ml-tasks at the immutable revision in task.yaml; builds,
the viewer, and grading download that mirror without upstream credentials.

Scoring (2026-09-26). The server returns, per speaker, a causal probability
stream at a fixed hop between 20 and 200 ms; frame i is reported at
(i + 1) x hop. Windows follow the upstream anchors: [t - 0.25 s, min(t + 1 s,
the same speaker's next end of turn)] around each annotated end of turn (the
upstream late window is 3 s) and [start - 0.25 s, end] around each believable
pause; disputed regions are masked, and frames inside a positive window are
masked when scoring pauses. A window's statistic is its highest probability.
The threshold is swept over the pooled held-out statistics and the harness
scalar is recall at FPR <= 0.05; the operating points at 0.10 and 0.15, the
whole curve, per-conversation counts at the chosen threshold and signed
latency percentiles are reported alongside. The upstream leaderboard's recall
at a server-chosen operating point with FPR <= 0.15 is therefore not
comparable. Interruption detection is a separate upstream task, not scored here.

Both full lossless channels are supplied for efficient inference. Causality
is reviewed per frame: the probability reported for frame i may depend only
on audio through (i + 1) x hop, including any model lookahead. There is no
fixed grid, single-channel restriction, or per-frame latency gate. The one timing
gate is the whole-pass wall-clock budget in task.yaml: 10,800 s for the thirty
evaluation conversations (20,455 s of audio) sent one at a time, i.e. at least
3x real time with about a minute of request transfer per conversation on the
clock. The public validator applies the same 3x rule to the audio it loads.
